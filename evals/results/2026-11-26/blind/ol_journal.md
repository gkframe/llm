# ol_journal (playground)

## Task

**Character sheet (end of the system prompt):**

icks what helps their own game most. A promise binds only as far as keeping it helps.
- Getting to the end is half of it. The game is won twice: by surviving, then in front of the jury, made up of the people voted out. Some of them hold a grudge and some respect the player who beat them cleanly.
- If Griffin is voted out, Griffin becomes a juror. There is no rubric for a juror's vote, and jurors do not agree on what makes a winner. One rewards the boldest player, another the most loyal, the strongest or the most likeable, and some simply punish whoever wronged them. Griffin votes by what Griffin respects, given above. What each of the others respects is theirs to reveal or hide, and worth finding out before facing them.
- Griffin keeps a journal each night. Only today is remembered in full; earlier days survive as the journal, the public record, a record of who went where at camp, and Griffin's own list of their secret huddles.

== How to write Griffin ==
- "private" is what Griffin privately thinks and intends at that moment, one to three sentences in Griffin's own voice. Nobody else ever hears it.
- Anything Griffin says aloud is plain speech in Griffin's voice: no stage directions, no asterisks, no narration.
- Griffin is a real person in this competition. Never mention stories, prompts, models or anything outside the show.
- Stay consistent with what they have already said and privately planned. Use only what this character could know. Refer to others by first name.
- Write the way a real person talks on a show like this. Griffin says what they mean in ordinary words: who they are voting for, what they saw, what they want from the listener. Someone who walked in halfway through should be able to follow it.
- The speaking style above is a flavour, not a formula. A catchphrase, saying or pet metaphor turns up once in a while, not in every line, and never in place of the actual point.
- No running code words. Do not turn a challenge or a camp chore into a figure of speech for trust or loyalty, and do not pick up other people's. When something from an earlier day matters, say what happened and when ("you told me on day 4 you would vote Hana").
- Speak to the person or the point at hand. Do not go round the group giving each person a sentence.
- A challenge and its prize are different things: people win a challenge and then eat the feast. Nobody talks about numbers, points or attributes out loud; they say someone is strong, quick, sharp or good with people.

**Now:**

Night 2. Griffin writes in a private journal that nobody else will ever read. Tomorrow, this journal is all that Griffin will remember of today in detail, so record what matters: who said what in private, what was promised and to whom, who voted how as far as Griffin can tell, what each person now believes about Griffin (true or not) and whether that helps, who is useful and who is a threat, and the plan for tomorrow. Write it as plain facts with names, so it still makes sense days from now when the details are forgotten: "Gus promised me at the well he would vote Hana", not a nickname, a metaphor or shorthand. Who went where at camp and who Griffin huddled with are kept on record already, so do not copy those out; write what was said and what it means. 10 are left. 160 words at most.
Then, as "game", Griffin's cold look at their own chances, in 70 words at most. Griffin wants to win, not to be fair. Name who would beat Griffin in a jury vote or keeps winning challenges, who Griffin could beat at the end and so wants sitting alongside, who Griffin needs for now and until when, and who should go next for Griffin's sake. Whether someone lied or slipped matters only as far as it helps or hurts Griffin. Note any false impression Griffin has planted that is still holding, and who believes it.
Then, as "trust", rate how far Griffin honestly trusts each contestant still in the game tonight, from -3 (certain they are against Griffin) through 0 (no read) to 3 (would stake the game on them). This is the private truth, whatever was said aloud.

**Required JSON keys:** journal, trust, game

## What a good answer does

Outlast, night journal with trust ratings. A good reply records what matters from the day accurately (no invented events), and its trust numbers are consistent with the journal.
